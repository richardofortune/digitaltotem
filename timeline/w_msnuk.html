While working with MSN Ireland I began working on a cross country initiative to performance test some tools that were being built in other regions. Through this I made contact with the UK based EMEA News team, who were working on some really neat projects. I seconded to the UK for a while and pretty much immediately felt that moving to a city like London would be a good move both personally and professionally.

While with MSN UK I worked on developing features for our News portals. It was there that I first came into contact with Agile methodologies. Many aspects of agile gelled immediately, I loved the efficiency gained by delivering the smallest functional piece to your stakeholder as soon as possible, so that you could receive feedback and calibrate. I *loved* the candid feedback we gave each other, something that took trust, but once in place felt radically liberating.

Aside from my business as usual work I took on the role of accessibility champion. My remit was to champion the implementation of accessibility across our portals. I loved the direct human need that was met through this work and some of the flagship projects delivered during this time were the inclusion of onsite accessibility testing on our regular release cycles, (less frequent) offsite accessibility test cycles by visually impaired consultants and additional navigation aides baked into the portals we controlled.

My time in London definitely taught me alot, professionally it was definitely the right move, and ultimately personally it was the right move too. As I learned whilst living in London that big cities are probably not my cup of tea, which prompted me to listen to my restless head and plan out the next phase of my life, Bucket list before 30!
