It was outside Xero that I first came up with the idea for Next Levels. I had dabbled with coaching in the past, and ever since then I'd always dreamed of an online tool that could help remote people work together to reach the next level in whatever area of their life they wanted to focus on.

The premise was simple. You identified the themes/areas you wanted to work on, you then imagined the highest level you realistically wanted to get to in that area, and you then mapped backwards from there the milestones or behaviours that got you where you wanted to go. I was also conscious that life does happen in isolation and thus decided that visualing levels in a radar graph was a great way to highlight when you might be overinvesting in one area over another. I created a version in google docs and used that with a friend. We worked using that for years.

While I was at Xero I saw people & teams burning out, losing sight of progress they'd made, or worse still losing sight of their desintation. I introduced the basics of Next levels to the QA team and we as a group began to map out the areas we wanted to level up in. We then derived our own individual areas for levelling up so that they helped us get to where we wanted to go as a team, but also enabled us to develop as individuals.

The initiative was very successful in the beginning and gave us a framework we could use to not only map out a path, but also see when we'd made progress. I received great feedback from individuals who found it helped them however ultimately as we exponentially grew as a team the culture around Next Levels diluted enough for the process to break down, and regrettably I didn't have the capacity to onboard new people and support it outside of my BAU workload.
