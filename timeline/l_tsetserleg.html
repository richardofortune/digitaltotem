A grand day for a walk

When I left Ireland to kick off on my bucket list I made a promise to myself that I'd continually seek to push past my comfort zone. When I arrived in Mongolia, I was already a little bit stretched and sensing my comfort boundary was near, I decided to undertake an adventure that would push me that much further. I hoped that when I returned things would feel comfortable again by comparison.

I decided I would go hiking in the middle of nowhere, so I grabbed a map and found a destination in the heart of the country. The destination I selected was called Tsetserleg. It was a 12 hour bus ride through the desert and once there the plan was rest up for a night and then walk into the wilderness and camp out for a night on my own. I would then pack up the next day and return to Ulan Bator with my new found sense of comfort! Easy! :)

The journey to Tsetserleg was really interesting. The foodstops and sights were really something else. The public toilets were an entirely different matter.

Once I arrived, I rested up and got my bearings. The next morning I was able to kick off on my hike. I aimed for the peak that overlooks the town. Passing wild dogs, circling birds of prey and mongolian cattle along the way, I found a site a few kilometers away and felt thoroughly great once I'd lit my fire and set up camp. Buoyed by my luck so far, I decided to try and climb a natural rock chimney I'd come across earlier that day. (I'm pretty bad with heights) It wasn't until I was about 10 meters up that I truly began to reconsider my choices. Why was I there, what was I trying to prove and what kind of mess would I make if I did take a fall. I managed to shimmy down safely and, although initially disappointed by what I considered to be fear, I eventually came to realise that it's all good pushing boundaries, but unless you have a goal in mind and are conscious of the impact it can have then you are probably not going to end up in the best possible place. Needless to say, I was glad I didn't make an impact on those rocks and managed to safely make it back to Ulan Bator, which seemed much more welcoming on my second return!
