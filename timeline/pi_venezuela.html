Hiking in Venezuela

In 2001 two friends and I decided to visit Venezuela. Overall it was the trip of a lifetime with so many great experiences. But one stand out memory for me was an experience I had whilst on a 5 day hike in the Canaima National Park.

It was the longest hike any of us had done and given our lack of experience we thought it prudent to hire a guide. Our guide took care of most of the details for the trip, we simply had to carry our own packs and our share of the equipment.

I recall as we hiked, that one of our party, the strongest of us, tended to carry a light pack, often intentionally selecting the lightest when we de-camped in the morning. I recall it really bothered me at first, that he'd not share the load equally. I recall consciously picking up some of the heavier bags so our guide wasn't left carrying an unfair share.

Thankfully, as is the way with hiking, you have lots of time to think. And thus I processed my take on fairness, my realistion that not all of us are the same or have the same values, but also that it is ok for us to be different, but that such differences don't preclude us having common agreements on behaviours.

We did end up discussing his preference for the light bags, and the consequence it had on the rest of us. We also went of to have many more great adventures together. It feels quaint and a little silly now, but that trip, that particular hike stands out in my memory as a pivotal moment in my life.
