Investing in your vision

In the early days of my adventures in maker community organising, I noted the challenges that lay in setting up maker groups. The challenge that came up time and time again was the cost involved in setting up a space. Secondary to that was the fear around ROI (especially in the case where funding was being sought). So, I set about creating a repeatable model that could not only illustrate the value and outputs of such spaces, but do so for a budget of $15,000.

After constructing an initial full size prototype in our own makerspace, I came to the conclusion that using a shipping container was the best the platform for our needs. It had the right balance between size and portability, demonstrating that a makerspace could work in any school, community centre or office.

I purchased a shipping container and a team from our makerspace undertook the work to make it usable as a makerspace. We had locally designed open-furniture, an opensource lasercutter and of course, opensource 3d-printers! It was a thing of beauty! We launched the Makercrate at the Makertorium event in 2013 and demonstrated to local council and educators that you don't need a massive amount of space to have a makerspace. We also demonstrated what could be achieved with such a reasonable budget.

In 2011, Christchurch experienced a devastating earthquake, which destroyed 50% of the buildings in the city centre. By 2013, people in Christchurch were looking to move past the quake, but real estate was at an insane premium. I worked with a team down there to deploy the Makercrate as a transitional project to help with the recovery. This installation went on to become the centrepiece of a transitional village in Christchurch and the team down there were able to secure $200,000 in funding thanks to the maker outreach work they did in the Makercrate.

Now in 2016, the Makercrate has moved to a new home in rural northern New Zealand and is once again acting as the foundation project in a new maker hub there. I'm immensely proud of the Makercrate; it was always a bit of a leap of faith, but I think it has achieved more than I could have ever hoped for!
