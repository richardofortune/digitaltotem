In 2006 I was just randomly browsing in a bookshop in Dublin. I don't recall why it caught my attention (the cover was brown and pretty dull), but I ended up stumbling across this book called Design for the Real World by Victor Papanek.

As I flicked through the pages something about the language resonated with me, and so I bought it. It was the first ever book on Industrial design that I'd ever bought. But once I began reading it, it felt more like a book about humanity than anything else. There were no shiny designs here, just chapter after chapter wonderfully human insights.

So much of what was presented in that book has permeated my thinking that it's near impossible to guage how many paths it has taken me on since reading. As it turns out the book is a design classic, and even now in a world where sustainability is very much part of our vernacular, it contains potent messages that can help to inform us.

I still get goosebumps when I think of the serendipity of the moment I found the book that would on to give me the language to articulate some of my greatest passions.
