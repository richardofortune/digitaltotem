In 2015 I decided to take up the 1000km-in-a-year, running challenge. I knew it wasn't really an endurance goal(regular runners can easily do that training for a marathon), but more of a commitment one, as I was ok at running, just terrible at running regularly.

In order to give my running a higher purpose I mapped the goal onto a project I'd wanted to try for some years.

The project's name was A Million Words for Charity. The idea was that I'd pick 5 novels that I liked and I'd carry them on every run with me. Essentially carrying 1 million words with me on every run, on behalf of those in the world who cannot read.

Logistically the books were a bit of a pain as my water bladders leaked on them more than once, so I ended up replacing them with a 1kg weight (called Puck!) which I brought with me on every run instead.

As the year progressed I heard of an old school friend on mine who had a charity in Nepal that had been affected by the April quake. I reached out to her and some old classmates to let them know I was doing this running thing and that I wanted to dedicate it to them. To try and raise funds to help in any way I could.

Months rolled by and my running was inconsistent at best, to the point where I had to double my weekly running efforts. By the end it was a Rocky'esque effort where I was running every other day, just Puck and me doing our thing, come rain or hail.

I finished my challenge on 31st Dec - it was a glorious day for a run and a fantastic way to round out the year. I raised a nice sum for my friends charity too, which felt really great.
