It was outside Xero that I first came up with the idea for Next Levels. I had dabbled with coaching previously and ever since then I'd always dreamed of an online tool that could help remote people work together to reach the next level in whatever area of their life they wanted to focus on.

The premise is simple. You identify the themes/areas you want to work on, you imagine the highest level you want to achieve in that area and you then map backwards from there, identifying the milestones or behaviours that get you where you want to go. I was also conscious that life doesn't happen in isolation and decided that visualing levels in a radar graph was a great way to highlight when you might be overinvesting in one area of your life over another. I created a version in google docs and used that with a friend and we worked with that for a few iterations.

While I was at Xero, I saw people and teams spin their wheels a little, losing sight of progress they'd made, or worse still, losing sight of their desintation. I introduced the basics of Next levels to the QA team and we as a group began to map out the areas we wanted to level up in. We then derived our own individual areas for levelling up so that we could contribute to where we wanted to go as a team, but also enabling us to develop as individuals as well.

The initiative was very well received and gave us a framework we could use to not only map out a path, but also to see when we'd made progress. This had the added benefit of us being able to identify gaps in our upskilling processes, which we could then address accordingly. I received great feedback from individuals who found it helped them. Ultimately, as we exponentially grew as a team, the culture around Next Levels diluted enough for the process to break down and regrettably I didn't have the capacity to onboard new people and support it outside of my BAU workload. Next Levels V.02 deserves some energy to circumvent this.
