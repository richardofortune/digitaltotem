Making it easy for Xero's to live, learn & laugh together

In 2014, Xero was already well into a phase of hyper-growth. As our staff numbers ballooned I wondered how we could on-board so many people and scale our culture. As a startup, we had a great culture of people just getting on and doing what needed to be done. This attitude obviously extended to social events, too.

I wanted to create a process that helped new people at Xero find their feet and and share their interests with other Xeros. Based on my experience with the Maker community, I noted that culture grows at the intersection between curiosity and passion. While it was easy for us "old-timers" to lead events, it was daunting for those who may never have done that sort of thing before. It was also daunting for anyone who was more introverted in nature.

I setup a closed Meetup group for Xeros and began by listing some events I could run. I started with 3d modelling and 3dprinting workshops. Given the public interest in these topics the events filled up quickly. Running them through meetup meant it was a lot less overhead for me to setup and host an event. I could easily communicate with those who were interested in this specific topic rather than clog up our other internal channels. All in all, it proved to be a really efficient model for organisiing events. From there I began actively inviting others to organise events, offering my support to make them happen. Pretty soon, we had a whole range of events happening, from Sticth and Bitch sessions to Cards Against Humanity to mountain biking to rock climbing. While the early adopters took to the process like ducks to water, I documented the system so that others could get involved, also reducing the need for me to be directly involved in the organising. We then saw other offices show an interest in Xero Social and the spread continued.

Aside from the core goal of making the organising of events easy, I also wanted to make the experience universal across Xero offices world-wide. That way, anyone visiting a region from another Xero office could easily find out what was on and get to meet other Xero's doing fun things. It also gave us a great way to guage interest in specific event types so that our HR team could then provide monetary support for social events in a really targetted way. Case in point, the 3d printing sessions I ran were so popular that Xero decided to invest in their own 3d printer. This now has its own group of users who work on projects all the time.

Xero Social continues to grow and I love hearing about new events. I particularly love how some people who were really shy in the begining are now running events for others. This hasn't just given people a fun time and a connection, it has also given the organisers confidence and skills in new areas, which gives me great confidence in the future health of Xero's culture.
