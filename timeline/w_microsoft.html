2001 - Microsoft

In 2000 I landed my dream job working for Havas interactive(AKA Blizzard) testing video games such as Echo the Dolphin and Counterstrike. It was a fantastic experience, as much fun as you'd expect, but unfortunately the role quickly began to feel like it lacked direction (there are only so many ways to make a Dolphin backflip) and so I began looking for an environment where I could grow.

Nowadays Microsoft might not have a name for being cutting edge. However, in 2001 MSN was doing really great things on the web and in Ireland the team were taking the MSN product and making it fit for consumption in 32 markets across the globe. I had found an opportunity to move from paddling in a comfortable stream to building strength in a roaring torrent, and as such I made the leap from the world of video games into the world of Microsoft Inc.

In my time at MSN I worked on various web and mobile projects, I managed vendor teams in Asia and it was under my watch that we deployed the Scandinavian version of MSN News to our French market. Not a fun morning in the office. We had made the assumption our non-european colleagues could inherently recognise the difference between French and Swedish, but we quickly learned and soon after, I implemented language cheetsheets for our offsite teams.

Whilst there I had the opportunity to setup the first automated mobile testing environment in our region and lead the technical side of our partnership between Vodafone DE and MSN. This partnership went on to win internal awards thanks to the test processes I put in place.

My time as a Quality Analyst at MSN was a blast. I loved applying my view of quality beyond localisation to other areas of our work. For instance, I felt our test infrastructure was not as good as it could be; it was fragile and retrograde. I championed and led the setup of our virtualisation environment, which not only saved us the equivalent of a years salary, but also massively expedited our test cycles. One of those projects that seems delightfully obvious in retrospect!

Microsoft took a great interest in developing its people. Thanks to this I was able to invest in my own growth, heavily using the internal library and access to personal development programmes during my time there. As a result, this really was a foundational time for me, resolving the belief that I could always grow a bit more! Ultimately it was this desire to grow that led me to my next adventure with MSN EMEA News Team.
